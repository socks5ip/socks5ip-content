# Buying Proxy IPs: Top 20 Questions to Ask Before You Pay

## Bottom line

**Ask about six topics — budget, type, protocol, quality, concurrency, after-sales — and test before buying. That sequence, in that order, is how you avoid the classic mistakes.**

## Budget and plans

**Q1: What does my budget buy?** Under ~10 CNY/month gets you datacenter dynamic (fine for scraping); 20–50 CNY reaches static or entry residential; above that, dedicated or high-purity resources. Chinese providers commonly start at 3–15 CNY/month.

**Q2: Short or long billing cycle?** Prove the workflow first, then buy long. Weekly/monthly for short projects; annual saves ~20–30% once the business is proven.

**Q3: What's the catch on ultra-cheap plans?** Usually shared exits, average purity, or concurrency limits. Test uptime and purity before letting the price tag decide.

**Q4: What does "IP count" in a plan mean?** The number of exits provided simultaneously. Scraping cares about count; account work cares about single-IP quality. Don't mix the two.

## Type selection

**Q5: Static or dynamic?** Need a consistent exit (whitelist, fixed identity, remote work)? Static. Need rotating exits (scraping, batch)? Dynamic.

**Q6: Residential or datacenter?** Residential = home-broadband resources, more "real", pricier. Datacenter = server resources, cheap. Account-sensitive work prefers residential.

**Q7: Is "static" the same as "dedicated"?** No. Static = the address doesn't change; it can still be a shared exit. Confirm "dedicated" explicitly if you need exclusivity.

**Q8: Domestic or overseas lines?** Match the exit to the business: domestic tasks need domestic exits; cross-border work needs the target country.

## Protocols and compatibility

**Q9: SOCKS5, HTTP, L2TP — which do I need?** SOCKS5 for apps/tools/games, HTTP for browsers/scrapers, L2TP for whole-device or router setups. Check what your software accepts.

**Q10: How do I know what my software supports?** Look at its proxy settings: fill IP:port + credentials → SOCKS5/HTTP; dial-up account → L2TP/PPTP.

**Q11: How many devices at once?** Ask about simultaneous connections. L2TP is usually device-unlimited; SOCKS5 is limited by concurrency/account authorization.

## Quality and purity

**Q12: How do I check purity?** Look up the ASN — ISP = residential, cloud = datacenter — then check blacklists; near-zero hits is clean.

**Q13: What uptime is acceptable?** ≥95% passable, ≥98% good for dynamic; test 50–100 connections and watch the failure rate.

**Q14: What latency is normal?** 20–80 ms domestic, 100–300 ms cross-border. High loss matters more than high ping.

**Q15: Will I inherit someone else's problems?** Shared exits carry association risk. Sensitive work → dedicated or residential.

## Concurrency and limits

**Q16: How do I calculate concurrency?** Concurrent tasks = concurrent exits needed. Multi-threaded scraping ≈ thread count; account work ≈ accounts operating simultaneously.

**Q17: How often does a dynamic line rotate?** Ask: per-request, timed, or manual. Long-lived sessions need long/manual cycles.

**Q18: Any traffic limits?** Most plans are unmetered; some cheap plans cap bandwidth or speed. Ask before heavy downloads.

## After-sales

**Q19: Can I get a refund if it's bad?** Ask about refunds and trial periods. Legit sellers support test-first; no test = red flag.

**Q20: Who do I contact when it breaks?** Test their support channel before buying — response time and working hours matter at 2 AM.
